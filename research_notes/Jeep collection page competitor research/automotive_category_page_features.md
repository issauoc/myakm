## Fitment/compatibility finders: how do RockAuto, Quadratec, ExtremeTerrain, CARiD, AutoZone implement "select your vehicle"?

### Takeaway
All of these retailers front-load a Year/Make/Model (YMM) vehicle selector near the top of the page/site (not deep in navigation), and the dominant pattern in the wider Shopify/aftermarket-parts ecosystem is for the widget to be powered by vehicle data stored against each product (metafields/ACES-style fitment data) so it can either (a) filter an entire category grid down to only compatible parts, or (b) persist as a saved "garage"/vehicle profile that then stamps a fit/no-fit flag on individual product cards site-wide. Confirmed, authoritative detail on the exact RockAuto/CARiD/AutoZone/ExtremeTerrain implementation was not obtainable without direct site access, so most specifics below come from secondary sources and should be treated as indicative rather than verified pixel-for-pixel.

### Cited Findings
- RockAuto's vehicle entry point is a year/make/model (and engine) selector positioned at the top of the page, and the catalog is explicitly built around "see all the parts we have for your vehicle" once a vehicle is selected — i.e., it filters the browsing experience vehicle-first rather than just flagging individual listings — per secondary write-ups: "Users enter vehicle information through the year/make/model selector at the top of the page" and "the unique RockAuto.com catalog makes it easy to see all the parts we have for your vehicle" — [stangnet.com forum summary](https://stangnet.com/mustang-forums/threads/rockauto-part-faq-search-made-easy.897840/?amp=1), [search-aggregated summary, low-confidence secondary sources](https://teleprompter.autocue.com/instagram15/rock-auto.html)
- RockAuto also supports "Vehicle favorites" that save vehicle info for faster repeat shopping (a saved-garage pattern) — [secondary aggregator summary, unverified against RockAuto's own help pages](https://zipdo.co/best/auto-parts-compatibility-software/)
- RockAuto's own Help documentation reportedly explains manufacturer-specific "left/right" terminology, implying fitment mismatches commonly stem from position/orientation ambiguity rather than pure year/make/model mismatch — referenced secondhand in a customer-review response — [customerlobby.com](https://www.customerlobby.com/reviews/5604/rockautocom/review/51722900)
- ExtremeTerrain's Tundra wheels category copy explicitly instructs shoppers: "choose the year, make, and model in the search option to ensure perfect compatibility, or reach out to one of our sales techs for help" — i.e., the YMM tool is paired with a human-assist fallback directly on the category page — [extremeterrain.com/tundra-wheels-tires.html](https://extremeterrain.com/tundra-wheels-tires.html) (via search snippet)
- ExtremeTerrain runs a persistent "Talk to a [Model] Sales Tech" call-to-action banner (phone number + hours, e.g., "1-877-870-8556, M-F 8:30A-11P, Sat-Sun 8:30A-9P") that appears across many Wrangler/Tacoma/Gladiator/Tundra category and product pages, dynamically labeled to the vehicle being browsed (e.g., "Talk to a Wrangler Sales Tech," "Talk to a Cherokee Sales Tech," "Talk to a Gladiator Sales Tech"), and at least one page offers a distinct "Connect Now" link alongside the phone option — [extremeterrain.com various category pages](https://extremeterrain.com/jeep-accessories-parts.html) (via search snippets)
- CARiD (operated by PARTS iD, Inc.) has expanded a "Shop by Service Type" feature covering "vehicle-specific product groupings for more than 500 repair tasks," which the company describes as enabled by "the company's robust vehicle fitment data" — a task/service-based fitment navigation layer distinct from plain YMM dropdowns — [PARTS iD press release via martechseries.com](https://martechseries.com/mobile/mobile-marketing/e-commerce-and-mobile-commerce/parts-id-inc-optimizes-customer-experience-with-expanded-shop-by-service-type-functionality/)
- A CARiD-affiliated forum thread (posted by CARiD staff) describes "TechDesk" specialists who help customers "find proper parts and verify fitment to avoid misfit and returns" — a human fitment-verification service CARiD surfaces to community forums, implying it is also offered pre-purchase, not just post-purchase — [gmsquarebody.com forum thread](https://www.gmsquarebody.com/threads/technical-questions-and-assistance-with-carid-techdesk.11385) and a related [jk-forum.com thread](https://www.jk-forum.com/forums/jk-write-ups-39/technical-questions-assistance-carid-techdesk-318202/)
- Amazon's auto-parts marketplace uses a green "Amazon's Choice"-style fitment badge inside its "Automotive Part Finder" that appears on listings carrying validated ACES (Aftermarket Catalog Exchange Standard)-compliant fitment data — cited as the reference model third-party fitment-tech vendors point to for what a trustworthy per-listing fitment badge looks like — [pcfitment.com vendor blog](https://pcfitment.com/blog/guide-to-display-the-amazonconfirmedfit-green-badge/) (vendor marketing source; treat as illustrative, not an AutoZone-specific finding)
- No direct documentation of AutoZone's own fitment-checker UI/badge was found in this search session; searches returned unrelated results (name badges, emblem products, generic eBay "confirm this fits your vehicle" disclaimers) rather than AutoZone's actual tool — Gap, see below.
- The general Shopify-app ecosystem for "shop by vehicle" fitment confirms the two structurally different behaviors the key question asks about: some apps **filter the whole grid** to only matching products once a vehicle is selected (e.g., Insane Vehicle Model Finder: "a customizable theme app block lets shoppers choose their vehicle and view matching products," with fitment data kept in product metafields), while others primarily **flag compatibility per product/listing** via a fitment table or indicator added to the product block (e.g., EasyYMM: "the YMM filter and per-product fitment widget install as theme blocks," PCFitment: "offers YMM search, fitment tables on product pages, and a dropdown for Year/Make/Model") — [Insane Vehicle Model Finder, Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/insane-vehicle-model-finder), [EasyYMM, Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/easyymm-year-make-model/easyymm-2), [PCFitment](https://pcfitment.com/shopify)
- A dedicated Shopify "automotive theme" (Spark) is built around a persistent "Shop by vehicle" entry in the site header, paired with a separate free app ("Spark Fitment") that supplies the underlying fitment data; per the theme's own docs, "without fitment data, compatibility badges and vehicle-lookup slots simply stay hidden," confirming that in this category the UI framework and the data layer are deliberately decoupled (so a Shopify store can ship the UI first and backfill fitment data incrementally) — [Spark Automotive Theme docs](https://automotivetheme.sparkshipping.com/docs/spark-fitment)
- Baymard Institute's 2026 Automotive Parts & Specialty UX benchmark (covering 10 sites including CarParts.com, Advance Auto Parts, RevZilla, TireRack.com, and ECS Tuning) concludes that "automotive parts sites consistently delivered strong ecommerce fundamentals, including clear category access, prominent vehicle-based product finders, pricing visibility, and technically stable checkout flows," but that these strengths "were often offset by gaps in comparison support and decision-stage clarity, critical factors in a fitment-dependent, high-risk purchasing environment" — i.e., even benchmark-leading sites nail the YMM finder but still commonly fail shoppers during side-by-side comparison and late-funnel reassurance — [Baymard automotive-parts-ux-benchmark-2026, via search snippet only; direct fetch blocked](https://baymard.com/blog/automotive-parts-ux-benchmark-2026)

### Inferences
- The consistent placement pattern across RockAuto, ExtremeTerrain, CARiD, and Shopify-native fitment apps is: YMM selector at or near the top of the page (header-level or hero-level), persistent/sticky across the session once set (via cookie/metafield-backed "garage"), with a human-assist fallback (phone/chat/TechDesk) offered alongside it specifically because YMM data coverage is never 100% complete and mismatches are the top cause of returns.
- The filter-the-grid vs. flag-per-product distinction is not either/or in practice — mature implementations do both: selecting a vehicle narrows the category grid, AND products that remain still carry an explicit "Fits your [Year Make Model]" / fitment-confirmed badge, which doubles as a trust signal even after filtering has already done the narrowing work.
- Fitment anxiety is resolved less by a single "blocking" mechanism and more by layering automated matching with an always-visible human-support option (sales techs, TechDesk), suggesting a Jeep parts collection page should not rely purely on metafield-based YMM filtering but also surface a low-friction "ask an expert" contact point near the filter.

- No direct, first-hand confirmation of RockAuto's exact sticky-header behavior, or whether its selector filters the entire catalog view vs. only flags individual listings — secondary sources describe a vehicle-first catalog model but don't specify sticky/non-sticky header mechanics. Needs direct site access.

## "Shop by vehicle generation/model" navigation: how do sites let Wrangler owners narrow to JK/JL/TJ/YJ?

### Takeaway
The clearest, best-evidenced pattern found is Quadratec's dedicated per-generation hub pages (e.g., a standalone page for "1987-1995 Wrangler YJ," another for "1997-2006 Jeep Wrangler TJ"), reached via a `/vehicle/{generation}` URL structure, functioning as generation-specific landing pages rather than a pure filter; this is complemented site-wide by product-level year-range tagging (e.g., "07-26 Jeep Wrangler JK & JL") that lets a single SKU serve multiple generations where parts are shared. A separate, different pattern — large clickable image tiles for "select your vehicle" — was confirmed on Carvana (a used-car marketplace, not a parts retailer) and only weakly evidenced (not confirmed) for parts sites; Rough Country instead uses a plain text "Browse by Models" list plus an "Add Your Vehicle" YMM dropdown rather than image tiles.

### Cited Findings
- Quadratec maintains dedicated generation hub pages with their own URLs, e.g. `quadratec.com/vehicle/1987-1995-wrangler-yj` and `quadratec.com/vehicle/1997-2006-jeep-wrangler-tj`, each of which in turn has its own sub-category pages (e.g., a "body-and-frame" sub-page scoped to the YJ: `quadratec.com/vehicle/1987-1995-wrangler-yj/body-and-frame`) — [quadratec.com/vehicle/1987-1995-wrangler-yj](https://www.quadratec.com/vehicle/1987-1995-wrangler-yj), [quadratec.com/vehicle/1997-2006-jeep-wrangler-tj](https://www.quadratec.com/vehicle/1997-2006-jeep-wrangler-tj) (via search snippets)
- Quadratec also publishes a standalone "Jeep Wrangler JK Owners Guide" catalog page, suggesting generation navigation is reinforced editorially (buying-guide content), not just structurally (category filters) — [quadratec.com/catalog/jeep-wrangler-jk-owners-guide](https://www.quadratec.com/catalog/jeep-wrangler-jk-owners-guide)
- Quadratec product listings are tagged with explicit, sometimes multi-generation year ranges rather than a single generation, e.g. a tail light assembly listed for "98-06 Jeep Wrangler TJ & Unlimited," a dead-pedal pad for "07-26 Jeep Wrangler JK, JL & Gladiator JT," and a spare-tire bike rack for "87-25 Jeep Wrangler YJ, TJ, JK & JL" — showing the generation taxonomy is applied at the SKU/fitment-tag level, letting one listing surface across several generation hub pages at once — [quadratec.com product listings, via search snippets]
- A Quadratec soft-top buyer's guide explicitly groups the line into three ranges for navigation purposes: "97-06 Jeep Wrangler TJ," "2007-2018 Jeep Wrangler JK 2 door & 4 door," and "2018+ Jeep Wrangler JL 2 door & 4 door" — confirming TJ/JK/JL is the generation vocabulary used customer-facing, not just internally — [quadratec.com soft-top buyer's guide, via search snippet](https://quadratec.com/c/video/best-wrangler-soft-top-2021-buyers-guide)
- Rough Country's navigation uses a "Browse by Models" text list (e.g., CJ5, CJ7, Cherokee, Comanche) plus an "Add Your Vehicle" control, i.e., a conventional list/dropdown rather than a visual image-tile grid — [roughcountry.com, via search snippet]
- Carvana's Jeep brand page uses an image-tile "Select your Jeep" layout where each model tile shows the model name, a starting price, and a live stock count (e.g., "Compass, from $31,845, 342 in stock") — a visual model-selector grid pattern, though Carvana is a used-car marketplace, not an auto-parts retailer, so this is an adjacent-industry reference point rather than direct evidence for parts sites — [carvana.com/new/jeep](https://www.carvana.com/new/jeep) (via search snippet)
- Kelley Blue Book's Jeep model section reportedly includes a "car-match quiz" alongside its model list, another adjacent (non-parts) pattern for helping a visitor self-identify their vehicle — [kbb.com, via search snippet, low detail]

### Inferences
- For a fitment-sensitive parts category, generation navigation appears to work best as a hybrid: a dedicated landing/hub page per generation (for SEO and editorial content — buying guides, generation-specific tips) combined with SKU-level multi-generation tagging (so a part that fits both JK and JL is discoverable from either hub without duplicate listings).
- The absence of confirmed image-tile "visual model selector" examples among actual parts retailers (vs. the Carvana/KBB adjacent-industry examples) suggests that for auto parts specifically, text-based generation hubs/dropdowns are currently more common than large visual tile grids — though this may simply reflect what surfaced in search rather than the full landscape.

### Gaps
- Could not directly inspect Quadratec's generation hub pages for whether they present a visual (image-based) generation picker at the top of the Wrangler category versus a plain text/dropdown list — only the existence and URL pattern of the hub pages was confirmed, not their exact on-page widget design.
- No confirmed example found of a major parts retailer (vs. a used-car marketplace) using a large clickable-image generation/model grid directly on a category page.
- ExtremeTerrain's and CARiD's specific generation-navigation UI (JK/JL/TJ equivalent) could not be confirmed — search only surfaced ExtremeTerrain's per-model "Talk to a [Model] Sales Tech" banners and per-model URL slugs (e.g. `/jeep-gladiator-winches-accessories.html`), not a generation-level (JK vs. JL) picker specifically.

## Build galleries / customer showcase content (UGC, "customer rigs," Instagram embeds)

### Takeaway
AmericanTrucks and AmericanMuscle (sister sites, same parent company as ExtremeTerrain/CARiD's category) run the most concretely evidenced customer-build program: dedicated "Customer Builds" content hubs plus a branded YouTube video series, with per-model customer-photo gallery pages (e.g., for GMC Sierra 1500, Dodge Ram) listing the specific parts used on a featured build. Direct confirmation that these galleries are "shoppable" (i.e., each part in the photo links straight to its product page) was not found in search results, though the pattern — a build page with "additional photos and product specs" — strongly implies a parts list is attached even if not confirmed as inline shoppable hotspots.

### Cited Findings
- AmericanMuscle produces a "Customer Builds" YouTube series; one 2018 feature highlighted a customer's Dodge Challenger GT build — [motorious.com coverage of AmericanMuscle's Customer Builds series](https://www.motorious.com/articles/news/2018-challenger-gt-build/), [motorious.com follow-up](https://www.motorious.com/articles/news/awesome-2018-challenger-gt-featured-on-americanmuscle-customer-builds-youtube-series/)

- AmericanTrucks hosts per-model customer-photo gallery pages, e.g. a GMC Sierra 1500 "photos & builds" page and a Dodge Ram "photos & builds" page, where copy describes customer builds as including "wheels, tires, trim pieces, and much more" — these pages also carry the same "Talk to a [Model] Sales Tech" CTA pattern seen on ExtremeTerrain — [americantrucks.com/gmc-sierra-photos-builds.html](https://www.americantrucks.com/gmc-sierra-photos-builds.html?from=9040), [americantrucks.com/dodge-ram-photos-builds.html](https://www.americantrucks.com/dodge-ram-photos-builds.html)

### Inferences
- The AmericanTrucks/AmericanMuscle pattern (same corporate family as several "parts enthusiast" retailers) shows build-showcase content is typically run as a semi-editorial program (video series + feature articles + per-model gallery pages) rather than a pure UGC-upload widget — i.e., it's curated/produced content, which likely performs better for trust/SEO than a raw unmoderated photo wall, at the cost of being slower to update.
- Tying a build-gallery photo back to a specific, purchasable parts list (as X-Cart's add-on does structurally) is the feature that converts a build gallery from "inspiration content" into a conversion tool; without that linkage, a gallery is closer to brand marketing than a merchandising block.

### Cited Findings

## Bundles/kits: complementary-product bundling and how it's merchandised on category pages vs. separate bundle PDPs

### Takeaway
Bundling of lift kit + wheels + tires (and sometimes shocks/floor liners/steering stabilizer) into a single named package is a confirmed, common pattern (Quadratec's "Aspen Package," an Offroad-Zone TeraFlex+Falcon+Koing+BFG bundle, Northridge4x4's wheel-and-tire "complete packages"), typically sold as its own distinct product/landing page with a name and a single price, rather than as an on-the-fly "frequently bought together" widget on the plain category grid. Several sources explicitly still tell the shopper to re-verify fitment even for a pre-bundled kit, reinforcing that bundling doesn't remove fitment anxiety — it just pre-solves the "which parts go together" question while leaving the "will this specific bundle fit my Jeep" question for the shopper to confirm.

### Cited Findings
- Quadratec sells an "Aspen Package" that bundles a lift kit (listed inconsistently in sources as "4-inch" in the marketing sentence vs. "2.5-inch" in the parts list — a direct internal inconsistency worth flagging) with 17-inch Drakon wheels, 35-inch Baja ATZ tires, floor liners, and a steering stabilizer, marketed as a solution to "conquer those vicious and hilly terrains," with a stated install time of 5–7 hours — [quadratec.com Aspen Package listing, via search snippet](https://www.quadratec.com/products/12055_0108.htm)
- Offroad-Zone bundles a TeraFlex 2.5" lift (for 4-door JK), Falcon shocks, Koing wheels, and BFG KO3 tires as a single packaged product priced at AED 16,740, with explicit copy telling the buyer to "verify compatibility with your specific model before purchase" even though it's sold as a pre-matched bundle — [offroad-zone.com TeraFlex/Falcon/Koing/BFG bundle](https://www.offroad-zone.com/en/teraflex-2-5-lift-with-falcon-shocks-koing-wheels-and-bfg-ko3-tires-bundle/)
- Northridge4x4 runs a dedicated "wheel & tire package deals" page (`/scp/wheels-and-tires/wheel-tires-package-deals`) with copy framing the value proposition explicitly as reduced shopping friction: "Simplify your Jeep's wheel and tire purchase by selecting one of our complete packages that offer a one stop shop for the new wheel and tire upgrade you've always wanted" — [northridge4x4.com wheel-tire package deals](https://northridge4x4.com/scp/wheels-and-tires/wheel-tires-package-deals)
- Individual lift kits are also still sold unbundled with explicit tire-size compatibility called out in the product copy itself (not a separate bundle), e.g. Rough Country's 1.75" JK kit ($449.99) stating it "allows you to mount up to a 33-inch tire," and Bilstein's B8 5100 kit for the JL stating Rubicon trims can run 35" tires while non-Rubicon models with stock fenders are limited to 33" — this "max tire size" callout functions as an informal fitment/compatibility disclosure embedded directly in standalone lift-kit listings — [realtruck.com Rough Country 1.75" JK lift listing](https://realtruck.com/p/rugged-ridge-1-75-lift-kit/rgr-18360-22/) (and related kit pages, via search snippets)

### Inferences
- The evidenced pattern treats a "kit" as its own merchandised product (named package, single SKU or small set of SKUs, own landing page) rather than a cross-sell carousel bolted onto the plain category grid — meaning for a Shopify Jeep collection page, the most directly evidenced approach is to create named bundle products/collections ("JL Rubicon Overland Package") rather than to rely solely on a generic "complete the look" app widget.
- Even bundled kits still carry fitment-disclosure language ("verify compatibility," explicit max tire size per trim), confirming that bundling is additive to — not a replacement for — fitment reassurance content.

### Gaps
- No confirmed example found of a bundle being merchandised inline inside a plain category grid (e.g., a highlighted "bundle" tile mixed in among individual product tiles) versus always living on its own distinct landing/product page — the sources consistently show the latter pattern, but absence of the former pattern in search results is not proof it never occurs.
- Could not confirm whether any of the five named flagship retailers (RockAuto, Quadratec, ExtremeTerrain, CARiD, AutoZone) use a "frequently bought together" style dynamic bundle widget on category pages specifically.
